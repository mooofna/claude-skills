# Linear Algebra for Data Science: course profile (synthetic example)

A made-up course that shows what a filled-in profile looks like. The slides in this folder were generated by
`tests/make_example_slides.py`.

## Course facts
- Weekly material: slides PDF on the course website (named `L<nn>-slides.pdf`), an exercise sheet with a solution one week later.
- The slides follow the lecture notes, chapter 3 for lecture 3.

## Exam
- Written, 120 minutes. One A4 sheet (front and back), typed at 11 pt or handwritten, is allowed.
- Old exams 2023 and 2024 with official solutions are published by the lecturer; 2025 is kept for a mock exam.
- Recurring question types: compute a low-rank approximation and its error; explain why a numerical method fails.

## What to emphasise in cards for this course
- Error formulas with their norms; why the SVD exists; numerical pitfalls.

## Summary sheet
- Rule: one A4 sheet, both sides, typed at 11 pt or more. Formulas that are long and easy to garble go on the sheet.
- Core identities that keep full-recall cards anyway: the SVD itself.

## Exam practice cards
- Release rule: after the exercise sheet on the lecture has been discussed in the tutorial.
- Held out for a mock exam: Exam 2025.

## Notation and conventions of the lecturer
- \(A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}\), singular values \(\sigma_1 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0\), \(A_k\) the rank-\(k\) truncation.

## Interference hotspots
- Spectral-norm error \(\sigma_{k+1}\) versus Frobenius error \(\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\sigma_i^2}\).
- SVD versus eigendecomposition (existence, bases).
